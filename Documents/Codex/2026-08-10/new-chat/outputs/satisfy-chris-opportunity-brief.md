# Satisfy Chris: the “golden prospect” opportunity brief

**Decision date:** 10 August 2026  
**Market scope:** UK-first specialist microbusinesses and small firms  
**Decision:** Validate **independent funeral directors** first with a narrow, human-approved arrangement-administration service.

## Executive answer

The best initial beachhead is **independent, mostly family-owned funeral directors handling roughly 15 or more cases per month**.

**One-sentence offer:** *“After each arrangement meeting, we turn your approved notes into the family confirmation, missing-information checklist, internal task list and supplier-message drafts—ready for a human to approve—so your team gets the paperwork under control without changing how it cares for families.”*

Why this wins:

1. **The pain is mundane and visible.** Every case creates sensitive, repetitive coordination and documentation.
2. **The market is reachable.** SAIF reports about **1,000 independent members across 1,865 sites**, and SAIFCharter publishes a prospect list ([SAIF](https://saif.org.uk/about-saif/), [SAIFCharter directory](https://www.saifcharter.co.uk/member-list)).
3. **There is a documented adoption gap.** A 2025 survey of nearly 300 mostly small independent funeral directors found continuing compliance pressure; firms cited administration fees, regulatory demands on time, and the cost and complexity of digital platforms ([Funeral Solution Expert](https://funeralsolutionexpert.co.uk/blog/fd-research-report-2025)).
4. **The service can be simple rather than “AI-native.”** It can sit beside the firm’s current process and produce drafts for human approval instead of demanding a system replacement.
5. **The main risk is manageable.** The product must never make care, legal, pricing, or cremation decisions. It drafts and organises; a named funeral professional approves every output.

Marine surveyors and legionella risk-assessment firms are credible runners-up, but both already have purpose-built reporting tools entering the market. That competition validates the pain while reducing the “blue sea” advantage.

## What Chris actually asked for

The source conversation defines six tests:

1. The owner understands the pain and offer immediately.
2. The outcome can credibly return **20 hours per month** or similarly obvious financial value.
3. A useful first version is deliverable in **three days or less**.
4. The workflow repeats across many customers in one segment.
5. Prospects are easy to identify and reach.
6. The niche is not already overwhelmed by equivalent offers.

This brief interprets “non-technical” operationally: the buyer should not need to understand AI, APIs, agents, or workflow software. Some candidates are technically skilled in their craft, but they run document-heavy businesses without dedicated software or operations teams.

## Scoring method

Each dimension is scored **1–5**. A 5 is strongest. For **Saturation**, 5 means few visible direct products; 1 means crowded. The ranking is an analyst judgment built from the linked evidence—not a statistic produced by the sources.

## Recommended beachhead design

### Ideal customer profile

An independent, family-owned funeral director with one to five branches, approximately 15+ cases per month, arrangements currently captured in Word, email, paper or a general funeral-management system, and no dedicated case administrator for every branch.

### Offer

**Arrangement Admin Autopilot:** after a human-led arrangement meeting, convert approved notes into a structured case summary, family confirmation draft, missing-information list, internal task checklist, supplier-message drafts and scheduled follow-up reminders—with explicit human approval before anything leaves the firm.

### Three-day MVP boundary

**Day 1 — one workflow, one template**

- Map one participating firm’s existing arrangement form and email templates.
- Define required fields, uncertainty flags, retention/deletion rules and prohibited outputs.
- Create secure case intake via typed notes, redacted transcript or form upload.

**Day 2 — draft and review**

- Extract case fields without inventing missing facts.
- Produce only five outputs: internal summary, missing-information checklist, family confirmation draft, internal task list and supplier-email drafts.
- Highlight every uncertain or absent field; require a named approver.

**Day 3 — export and test**

- Export to email/PDF or copy into the firm’s existing system; no deep integration.
- Test with at least five synthetic or fully redacted historical cases.
- Record creation time, review time, error types and percentage of generated text accepted.

**Explicitly out of MVP:** automated family messaging, pricing decisions, legal advice, cremation-form submission, care instructions, obituary publication, payment handling, supplier booking, or replacement of the firm’s system of record.

### Pricing and ROI model

**Validation offer:** five-case, two-week assisted pilot at no charge in return for timestamps and structured feedback.  
**Commercial hypothesis:** **£249/month for up to 15 cases**, then £12 per additional case.

Illustrative monthly value:

- 15 cases × 80 minutes saved = **20 hours**.
- At a loaded/admin opportunity value of £20/hour: **£400 value**, £151 net after £249 fee.
- At a director/arranger opportunity value of £35/hour: **£700 value**, £451 net after fee.

Both hourly values and 80-minute savings are assumptions. The pilot must replace them with observed data. Do not advertise “20 hours saved” until the measured median supports it.

### Seven-day validation plan

| Day | Action | Evidence produced | Pass threshold |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Select 20 SAIFCharter prospects across single-branch and small multi-branch firms; prepare a non-technical interview script. | Prospect sheet + six-question script | 20 named firms, no generic inbox-only list |
| 2 | Request 15-minute workflow interviews; lead with “reduce arrangement paperwork,” not AI. | Response log | 5 interviews booked from 20 attempts, or revise message once |
| 3 | Interview owners/arrangers; map exact documents, systems and handoffs. | Current-state workflow maps | At least 3 firms report ≥60 minutes of post-meeting admin per case |
| 4 | Ask three firms to walk through one synthetic or redacted case; time every step. | Baseline timestamps | Median addressable work ≥75 minutes/case |
| 5 | Show static examples of the five outputs; measure edits and trust objections. | Acceptance/error log | ≥80% of draft fields accepted after review; zero invented critical facts |
| 6 | Present £249/month and a five-case assisted pilot. | Price reactions + pilot intent | At least 2 firms accept pilot and at least 3/5 view £249 as plausible if savings hold |
| 7 | Decide: proceed, change wedge, or move to runner-up. | One-page decision memo | Proceed only if two pilots, addressable median ≥75 min/case, and no unsolved privacy/process blocker |

**Stop conditions:** pause and ask rather than “grind” if firms will not provide even redacted/synthetic cases; if existing software already produces all five outputs acceptably; if median addressable time is below 45 minutes per case; or if data handling cannot be agreed without disproportionate security/integration work.

### Positioning Chris can use

Do not say: “We built an AI funeral operating system.”  
Say: **“You care for the family; we organise the approved paperwork after the arrangement.”**

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Sensitive personal and bereavement data | UK-hosted processing where possible, data-minimisation, written retention/deletion policy, no model training on client data, access logs and redacted pilot cases. |
| Hallucinated facts | Structured fields, “unknown” as a valid answer, source traceability, uncertainty highlighting and mandatory human approval. |
| Harmful tone in family communication | Firm-approved templates, no automatic send, named reviewer and a banned-phrase/tone checklist. |
| Scope creep into legal or care decisions | Explicit prohibited-output list; generate administrative drafts only. |
| Existing-system resistance | Begin with email/PDF exports and copy/paste; integrate only after measured demand. |
| Weak economics at low volume | Qualify for approximately 15+ cases/month or sell per-case; do not force a subscription on very small firms. |

## Final recommendation

**Run the seven-day funeral-director validation first.** It best matches Chris’s requirement for a simple, repeatable, non-AI-branded service with an obvious human outcome. Keep marine surveyors as the second test if funeral firms cannot clear the trust/data bar.
